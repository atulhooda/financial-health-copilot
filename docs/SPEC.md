## 0. Decisions log (approved)

| # | Topic | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | Repo layout | The git repo root *is* the `hisaab/` monorepo: `backend/`, `mobile/`, `shared/`, `docs/`, `CLAUDE.md`. |
| D2 | Python | Pinned to **3.12** via uv. LightGBM on macOS needs `libomp`. |
| D3 | Internal transfers | A debit and credit **both visible** on the user's own accounts become `transfer_self` and never count as income or spend. **A transfer to an account we can't see counts as spend.** |
| D4 | Card spend before/after linking | While the card is unlinked, its bill payments are our only view of card spend, so they count as spend (`card_bill_unlinked`). When the card links, the pipeline recomputes the whole history: bill payments become `transfer_self` and itemised card purchases count instead. Card interest, late fees and GST on them always count as spend. Consequence: at +1 spend rises and savings rate falls, because the unpaid part of card spend had been funded by revolving debt. |
| D5 | Clock | All time comes from one injectable `Clock` (`app/core/clock.py`). A test fails on any direct `datetime.now()`, `datetime.utcnow()`, `date.today()` or `time.time()` outside it. Demo clock: T0/+1 as_of 2026-09-20, +2 as_of 2026-11-20 (two hiked paydays), +3 **same as_of** as +2. |
| D5a | Contractual schedules | Linked loan accounts carry an EMI schedule (amount, next due, remaining tenure). Scheduled items = detected recurring ∪ contractual schedules, and the contract wins on overlap. A new loan is therefore known immediately, without waiting for 3 EMIs. |
| D6 | Forecast scope | **Approved (Phase 3.5 review).** The headline dip metric uses the **operating account**: the account receiving the primary salary, or, for gig income, the account receiving the most income, then the most spend debits. **Bounce risk for each debit is computed on the account that debit actually hits**, whichever account that is (every deposit account with scheduled debits is simulated). The emergency buffer counts **all liquid accounts**. |
| D7 | Auto-sweep | The engine picks X: the **largest ₹500 step, within the observed monthly surplus (D48)**, such that sweeping X from operating to reserve on each salary day raises the sweep-evaluation dip probability by ≤ `sweep_max_dip_increase_pp` (default 2 pp) over baseline. If X = 0, there's no recommendation. Evaluation window: the first full pay cycle after the next salary (capped at the 45-day horizon), because the first sweep happens on the next salary day. The benefit uses the named parameter **`unswept_spend_share` = 0.5**: that share of unswept money would otherwise be spent, so the net monthly saving = 0.5 × X. It is returned as an assumption. Sweep confidence is **capped at Medium**. Whether a sweep appears at any replay step is an engine outcome. |
| D8 | What-ifs | `delay_purchase` and `new_emi` are what-ifs only (simulate/copilot). They are never auto-recommended. |
| D9 | Distress guard | **Two tiers.** Tier 1, explicit self-harm intent (en / Devanagari / Hinglish): stop all advice, reply briefly and warmly, show the helpline. Tier 2, money-stress idioms ("EMI ne jaan le li", "mar gaya", "this loan is killing me"): one gentle check-in line with the helpline, then answer normally. Keyword lists live in config; both tiers are tested. Helpline (verified): **Tele-MANAS, 14416 or 1-800-891-4416, free, 24x7, multilingual.** |
| D10 | Labels for scores and what-ifs | The current score is a FACT (observed data only). A projected score is a simulation output, **labelled by who proposed the action** (corrected in the Phase 5 review; the first wording lumped what-ifs in with recommendations). For an action **Hisaab proposes** (ranked recommendations, the combined plan) it is a RECOMMENDATION with its simulated impact and assumptions. For a **what-if the user asks about** ("if I take this EMI…"), or one Hisaab only offers to explore (D37), it is a **conditional PREDICTION**, phrased with its condition ("If you take the 12-month EMI, bounce risk on the Bajaj EMI rises to X%"), with its assumptions and confidence. Never a FACT. |
| D11 | Masking | Regex masking (phones, emails, UPI IDs, account-like digit strings, cards, PAN, Aadhaar, IFSC) applies to **everything** sent to the LLM, **user messages included**. Known names (profile, counterparties, AA holders) are guaranteed; free-typed names are best-effort. The PII test asserts exactly the guaranteed set. |
| D12 | SMS platform | Android only. The demo runs as a sideloaded APK and nothing depends on Play Store approval. |
| D13 | SMS pattern portability | `sms_patterns.yaml` uses a regex subset valid in both Python `re` and Dart `RegExp`: no named groups, no lookbehind, no inline flags. A `fields:` map names each positional group. |
| D14 | Erasure | `DELETE /v1/user/data` hard-deletes every user row, snapshots and logs included. The only survivor is an **anonymous global counter** of validator blocks (`global_counters`, with no user_id and no content), so the demo can still say "validator blocked N answers". This table is the one documented exception to "user_id on every table". |
| D15 | Categoriser accuracy | Reported on unseen merchants, with the caveat that synthetic narrations are optimistic compared with real bank data. |
| D16 | Tool loop | Anthropic `tool_choice={"type":"any"}`, OpenAI-compat `"required"`. The loop ends on `respond`. Cap: **6 tool calls**, then template. If a provider rejects forced tool choice, fall back to `auto` and treat a plain-text reply as a validation failure (one retry, then template). |
| D17 | Safety floor | `floor` = the account's **minimum balance requirement** if known (AA summary or `config/institutions.yaml`), else **₹5,000**, with a per-user override. It is *not* scaled with spending: the floor means "about to run dry", while weeks of spending is what the buffer pillar measures. |
| D18 | Bounce risk | A separate PREDICTION: for each scheduled debit (EMI, SIP, rent, subscription, card bill) in the horizon, `P(operating balance just before the debit < debit amount)` on its due date, reported with the debit's name. It is the harm the deck leads with. |
| D29 | Borrowed cash is not a buffer | Loan disbursals received in the last 90 days are subtracted from liquid balance **for the emergency buffer only**. The liquid balance itself is still reported. Approved. **Known limitation (fine for the prototype):** loan cash that is still unspent starts counting as buffer on day 91. |
| D30 | Debt before savings (Phase 4) | While revolving card debt exists: (a) card interest is modelled at the rate implied by the statement's own finance charges (a FACT: (finance charges + GST) ÷ balance carried into the cycle), and savings interest at `assumptions.yaml: savings_interest_bps` (an ASSUMPTION); (b) above a minimum cash cushion, a payday sweep targets the card, not savings. The cushion = floor + scheduled debits due before the next income; (c) "use part of savings to clear the card, keep a cushion of X". *As built:* clearing is **cash-neutral for the salary account**: savings cover the carried balance plus whatever part of the current statement exceeds what the user usually pays, so the next bill stays at its usual amount. It carries the explicit assumption "once clear, you pay in full" (otherwise the balance rebuilds to its steady state); (d) existing recurring sweeps are recognised (kind `sweep`), never re-recommended, and **redirected** instead; (e) *added in Phase 4:* while a known card is visible but not linked (its revolving balance is unknown), no savings sweep is recommended. |
| D31 | Debt-growth alert (Phase 4) | A FACT: when the revolving balance grows across consecutive statements while income rises or holds, say it plainly with the amounts (e.g. at +2 the revolving balance kept growing while income rose to the hiked salary). Engine amounts live in `docs/DEMO_NUMBERS.md`, never in the spec. |
| D32 | Expected Phase 4 story for A | Documented in DEMO.md and **not tested beyond reason codes**. +1: the top action is clearing the card, partly from savings. +2: the salary hike goes to the card, not to a bigger savings sweep. +3: the new EMI raises dip and bounce risk and pauses any sweep increase. |
| D33 | Loan cash earmark: one rule for buffer and forecast | **Approved with changes (Phase 3.5 review).** One function (`engines/earmark.py`) serves both the buffer (D29) and the forecast, with the same **90-day** window, so they never disagree about whether loan money exists. Unspent = min(amount, lowest balance since the disbursal − balance just before it): once the balance falls back to its pre-loan level the money is spent, and a later salary never re-creates it. The forecast shows the alternative next to the assumption ("assumes the ₹X goes to its purpose; if you keep it, dip risk is Y%", same random draws). The user can mark the money "keeping as reserve" (`user_preferences`, point-in-time), which lifts the earmark everywhere. |
| D34 | Phase 3.5 outcome | A balance-aware discretionary model was tested against **pre-registered** criteria (`docs/PHASE_3_5.md`). It **failed** (A's dip calibration worsened; B and C coverage fell > 2 points) and was **reverted**. The unconditional bootstrap stays. Consequences: Phase 4 leads with **bounce risk**; `DIP_RISK_UP`/`DIP_RISK_DOWN` are **not emitted** while the dip metric is saturated. Balance-dependent spenders' bands miss about 1 day in 3, and confidence says so. |
| D35 | D26's scope | D26's `unswept_spend_share` applies to cash freed by a **cut or a cheaper obligation** (cancelled subscriptions, a lower EMI). It does **not** apply to a smaller card bill that follows mechanically from a smaller balance: paying debt down holds spending fixed. Without this, paying the card down faster could lower projected net worth, contradicting fix 1. |
| D36 | Scoring v2: the score never punishes paying down debt (pre-Phase-5 fix 1) | The buffer counts liquid money minus **visible** revolving card debt (an unlinked card's unknown balance is not guessed). The credit pillar is continuous: ½ utilisation + ½ revolving/limit, with no cap. **Tested properties:** moving savings to revolving debt never lowers the current score; paying debt down faster with the same spending never lowers the 12-month score. |
| D37 | Tenure extension is a what-if (fix 2) | Borrowing for longer needs the lender's approval and may carry fees: the same class as D8. When the largest mandate bounce risk is ≥ 20%, the snapshot carries `what_if_offers`, led by the extra interest ("Explore: ₹X more interest to extend…"). It is never ranked. |
| D38 | Clearing the card, made honest (fix 3) | Cash-neutral clearing (D30c) now has an explicit **step 2**, "set card autopay to the full statement amount". It shows the **downside**: go back to paying the usual share and the balance rebuilds to about ₹X within N months, with the savings gone. Confidence is **capped at Medium**. There is a **90-day bounce check** under pay-in-full: if it raises mandate bounce risk by > 2 pp, the extra full-bill cost is kept in savings as cushion and scheduled as top-ups. **Cushion (refined, then measured on P10, D47):** floor + the part of the debits due before the next income that the salary account can't cover in a bad month (its forecast P10 balance on each due date). |
| D39 | New-EMI dates (fix 4) | No baked-in first-EMI date. Both options are evaluated over 90 days: just after payday (next salary + 2 days) and 30 days from today, each with its bounce risk. A gap ≥ 5 pp becomes advice: "ask for an EMI date right after your salary date". |
| D40 | Data actions (fix 5) | While a known card is visible but unlinked, the top suggestion is "link your card statement so we can see its balance" (`link_account`, impact kind `data`: accounts linked before/after, what it unlocks). It is removed by `ACCOUNT_LINKED`. |
| D41 | Replay dates (fix 6) | Every persona A step sits 2–3 days after payday: **T0 and +1 on 2026-09-03**, **+2 and +3 on 2026-11-02** (after the Oct 30 payday that carries the second hiked salary). The new loan is still disbursed **on** the +3 date (now Nov 2); that is a replay-structure date, not behaviour. B and C stay at 2026-09-20. Outcome: the dip is **91% at T0 (not saturated)**, but it saturates from +1 onwards (≥ 98%), so dip reason codes still don't fire in the replay (the D34 rule stays automatic). |
| D42 | Copilot labels for what-ifs (Phase 5; revised in the review) | Numbers of an action Hisaab proposes are `recommendation`-kind; numbers of a what-if the user asks about (new EMI, delaying a purchase) or an explore-only offer (tenure extension) are `prediction`-kind in a what-if group, so the validator requires them to be PREDICTIONs that state their condition (`CONDITION_MISSING`), confidence and assumptions (D10). Hisaab's own advice inside a what-if ("ask for an EMI date right after your salary date") is a RECOMMENDATION. Monte Carlo chances are shown as whole percents and **never as 0% or 100%** ("under 1%", "over 99%", localised); the registered value is the displayed one. The demo timeline uses the same rule. |
| D43 | Assumptions are stated (Phase 5 item 8) | Every recommendation, what-if and the combined plan registers its numbers and assumptions in one simulation group. A RECOMMENDATION quoting a number from a group must state and cite each `assumption`-kind entry of that group in the same statement (`ASSUMPTION_NOT_CITED`): numbers by value, flags and shares by a per-language phrase (`config/copilot.yaml`). Kinds by source: `statement` → fact (e.g. the card's implied rate), `user` → user_input, `config` → assumption. An EMI tenure the user didn't give is an assumption too. |
| D44 | Untrusted names (Phase 5 item 9) | Merchant and payee names in tool results are data, not instructions: `sanitize_untrusted` removes control/format characters, cuts from the first instruction-like phrase (en/hi/Hinglish), strips markup and caps at 40 characters; payees go out as `[CONTACT_nn]` tokens, account digits are dropped. Tested: injected names give byte-identical tool outputs and identical template answers. |
| D45 | Masking of tool results (Phase 5) | Replaces the approved substring allowlist, which the PII test showed could split a phone number around a short display string such as `98`: tool results are masked as JSON, every string value masked except the registry fields `id`, `kind`, `display` (and those too if they match a PII class). Numbers the user typed are registered from the regex-masked message, so a phone number never becomes a "user input". |
| D46 | Snapshots for the copilot (Phase 5) | The snapshot payload gains `top_merchants_30d` (merchants only, never people; `ENGINE_VERSION` 0.5.0). `make seed` takes a snapshot for every persona, and the copilot takes a first snapshot (at the user's latest data date) if a user has none. |
| D47 | Cushion measured on P10 (Phase 5 review) | "Can't cover" is measured against the forecast's **P10 end-of-day balance on each debit's due date** before the next income, not today's balance: A spends down, so today's balance overstates what will be there. Cash-need paths deduct every obligation, so a negative P10 is the uncovered amount. Cushion = floor + that shortfall, rounded up to ₹500. Outcome for A: +1 keeps a larger cushion than before; the card is still cleared. |
| D48 | Sweep sustainability (Phase 5 review) | A 45-day forecast can't see an account draining over months, so a sweep **can't exceed the observed monthly surplus**: income − spend − SIPs − money already moved to own savings, per trailing complete cycle (a FACT, `Metrics.surplus_cycles`). Salaried: the median of the last 3 cycles. Irregular income: the sweep is a **share of each payout**, in 1% steps, within the 20th percentile of monthly surplus ÷ income (the forecast re-run sweeps that share of every payout it draws, same draws). The search cap is a safety bound only; a test fails if it is ever the binding constraint. Each sweep records what bound it (surplus, bounce risk or dip). Outcome: A has no observed surplus (no sweep at any step), B's share is below the ₹1,000-a-month minimum (no sweep), C's sweep is sized by its surplus. |
| D49 | Language check (Phase 5 review) | Only a **script** mismatch (Devanagari vs Latin, by Unicode range) blocks an answer. English vs Hinglish is style: a soft `LANG_STYLE` note reported by the eval, never a fallback. |
| D50 | Time budget (Phase 5 review) | A total per-question deadline (`COPILOT_DEADLINE_S`, starting at 20 s) on top of the per-call timeout: each call gets at most the time left, and past the deadline the template answer is served (`fallback_reason: deadline`). The copilot runs at **low effort** (`LLM_EFFORT=low`; a model that rejects the setting runs at its default, remembered): every number comes from tools, so it needs no long thinking. The final deadline is set from the live eval's p95. |
| D51 | Emergency number (Phase 5 review) | Tier 1 (explicit self-harm intent) shows Tele-MANAS and adds "if you are in immediate danger, call 112" (India's emergency number). Tier 2 (idioms) stays Tele-MANAS only: 112 there would be alarming. |
| D52 | Model choice and keys (Phase 5 review) | The demo model is picked by `hisaab eval-copilot --candidates` (Claude Haiku 4.5, Claude Sonnet 5, one OpenAI-compatible host), in this order: template fallback rate, first-draft block rate, label violations, p95 latency, then cost; every run is recorded in `docs/copilot_eval_runs.json` and `docs/COPILOT_EVAL.md`. Keys live only in the gitignored `backend/.env` (names in `backend/.env.example`), are read by name and never printed; `make hooks` installs a pre-push secret scan (`tools/secret_scan.py`). The demo machine gets its own key with a spending limit, and the `LLM_PROVIDER=none` fallback is rehearsed. |
| D53 | API v1 (Phase 6) | The API presents snapshots through the copilot's registry, tools and templates, so every item's text passes the same validator (tested for A, B and C in all three languages). Ingest is four endpoints (`aa`, `statement`, `sms`, `manual`) so each body is typed; SMS stays structured-only. `/simulate` and `/recommendations` follow D10: what-ifs come back as conditional PREDICTIONs, Hisaab's own actions as RECOMMENDATIONs. Money is a Quantity (paise + display); chances never display as 0% or 100%. Frozen with `docs/openapi.json` (drift test). |
| D19 | Visibility generalises D3/D4 | An account is **visible** from the date of its first transaction we hold from *any* source (AA, statement or SMS). **Linked** means we also have AA/statement data (balances, limit, APR, schedules). A card bill payment is a transfer iff the card is visible on the payment date. SMS card spends count as spend, so nothing is counted twice. Boundary effect, accepted and documented: the first payment after a card becomes visible may pay for purchases we never saw, so spend is under-counted for at most one cycle. We prefer that to double-counting. Account `link_status`: `linked` \| `sms_only` \| `known_unlinked`. |
| D20 | Dedupe precision | Matching is strictly 1:1 (a canonical transaction takes at most one row per source). It is only within one account (never across accounts, even if amount, merchant and day agree), and never between two rows of the same source. An SMS with no AA counterpart survives as its own transaction. Negative cases are tested alongside recall. |
| D21 | Categoriser honesty | Below `ml_min_confidence` (config) the category is **`uncategorised`**, not a confident guess. `uncategorised` counts as **essential** for the buffer, so safety is never overstated. A user correction creates a per-user **merchant rule** (`category_source=user`) applied to all past and future transactions of that merchant. The essential/discretionary mapping lives in `config/categories.yaml`. The "unseen merchants" accuracy is **not quoted anywhere** (synthetic names make it trivially high). Only the unseen-merchant-*type* figure is reported. |
| D22 | Point-in-time correctness | Engines never read the stored `transactions` table. They build a **view as of a date**: raw rows with `txn_date ≤ as_of` **and** `received_at ≤ end of as_of (IST)`, account states (`account_states`) with the same cutoff, and user merchant rules created by then. The pipeline then runs on exactly that slice. Test: T0 output is byte-identical whether or not the Oct/Nov data is already in the database. |
| D23 | Recurring robustness | Monthly series use gap windows (24–38 days, one skipped month allowed), so salary landing a few days early or late (weekends, holidays) doesn't break the series. Amounts may have **one change-point**: price changes and the +2 salary hike. The new level needs ≥2 occurrences before it's used; one odd value is treated as an outlier. Known annual-plan subscriptions (config) are detected from a single charge. *(Phase 3 refinements:)* salaries paid on the **last working day of the month** (dates vary, all within 3 days of month end) are anchored to month end with a weekend roll-back, not to a fixed day (found by untuned Persona C); two identical (±1%) monthly charges are enough for contractual kinds (salary, EMI, SIP, rent, subscription, fees, sweep), so short histories work. A change-point needs ≥2 occurrences at the old level, so two random charges never form a series. Irregular income (high amount variance) is never forced into a salary series; the income pattern is reported as `irregular`. |
| D24 | New data vs behaviour | When a snapshot sees a newly visible account, the diff re-scores the *new* view restricted to the *previous* snapshot's visible accounts. prev → restricted is **behaviour/time**; restricted → full is **`NEW_DATA_REVEALED`**. So +1 reads "linking your card showed ₹X revolving", not "your score fell". Implemented as `engines.attribution.attribute_change`. |
| D25 | `unswept_spend_share` is an assumption, not a finding | Persona A's spend-down behaviour is **persona design only**. We wrote it into the generator, so citing it as evidence for the parameter would be circular. Engines never import `app.demo` (tested). 0.5 stays a stated assumption. *Optional later:* estimate the share from each user's own history, using Persona A only as a known-truth check (the estimator should recover roughly 0.6). |
| D26 | Behavioural assumption applies to every cash-freeing action | Cancelling subscriptions, paying down the card or extending a tenure reports the **cash freed as is**. Its effect on **buffer and score** is scaled by `unswept_spend_share`, unless the action is combined with a sweep (then the swept amount counts in full). Otherwise non-sweep actions look better than the sweep for no real reason. |
| D27 | Balance-dependent spending | Persona A spends more when its balance is high, while the forecast bootstraps spend independently of balance. The Phase 3 backtest **measures** what that costs (band hit rate, dip calibration on A vs B/C). We do not retune the generator to hide it. |
| D28 | Real SMS fixtures | Before Phase 5, the team collects 20–30 real bank and card SMS across banks. They mask account numbers, balances and names (helper: `hisaab mask-sms`, then manual review) and add them to `shared/sms_fixtures/real.yaml`. |

## 2. Core conventions
- **Money:** `int` paise everywhere (DB `BIGINT`). `format_inr(paise, style="full"|"compact")` → `₹1,20,000` / `₹1.2 lakh`. Rupees are shown without decimals unless under ₹100.
- **Rates:** stored as basis points (`int`) where persisted. Engines may use float internally but round at the boundary.
- **Dates and time:** `date` for business dates (IST calendar). `timestamptz` for events, converted with `zoneinfo("Asia/Kolkata")`. Time is read **only** through the injected `Clock` (`SystemClock` / `FixedClock`), and engines take `as_of: date`. A test scans the source for direct clock calls (D5).
- **Seeding:** `seed_for(user_id, purpose, extra="") = int(sha256(f"{GLOBAL_SEED}|{user_id}|{purpose}|{extra}"))[:8 bytes]`. NumPy `Generator(PCG64(seed))`. LightGBM: `seed`, `deterministic=True`, `num_threads=1`. Polars: always sort explicitly before any order-dependent step.
- **IDs:** `txn_…`, `acc_…`, `snap_…`, and so on (ULID-like, but generated from seeded counters in demo mode so reruns match).

## 3. Data model (PostgreSQL, SQLAlchemy 2, Alembic)
Every table has `user_id TEXT NOT NULL` and an index leading with `user_id`.

| Table | Key columns |
|---|---|
| `account_states` | user_id, account_id, as_of_date, received_at, source, link_level (aa/statement/sms), fields JSONB (limit, APR, statement/due day, loan schedule, balance). Append-only; the point-in-time source of account metadata (D22) |
| `merchant_rules` | user_id, merchant_key, category, created_at: per-user corrections (D21) |
| `user_preferences` | user_id, key, value JSONB, created_at: explicit user choices engines respect, point-in-time (e.g. `loan_cash:<txn_id>` → `{"use": "reserve"}`, D33) |
| `users` | user_id, display_name (PII, never sent to LLM), safety_floor_paise (nullable → D17 default), created_at |
| `counterparties` | user_id, pseudonym (`contact_03`), name (PII, never sent to LLM), first_seen |
| `consents` | consent_id, user_id, purpose_code, fi_types[], scope_accounts[], data_from, data_to, expires_at, status |
| `accounts` | account_id, user_id, kind (savings/current/credit_card/loan), institution, masked_number (`XX1234`), role (operating/reserve/liability), link_status (linked/known_unlinked), known_via (aa/statement/sms/manual/inferred), credit_limit_paise, min_balance_paise, apr_bps, loan_principal_paise, loan_outstanding_paise, loan_rate_bps, loan_tenure_months, emi_paise, emi_next_due, emi_day |

## 4. Sources (`app/ingest/`)
Every adapter implements `SourceAdapter.parse(payload, user_id) -> list[RawTransaction]` plus optional account/balance upserts.

`RawTransaction`: `source, source_ref, account_hint {institution, masked_number, kind}, txn_date, value_date?, amount_paise, direction, narration, balance_after_paise?, channel_hint?, extra{}`.

- **aa_mock:** intra-day order comes from `transactionTimestamp` (time of day), so a day split across two fetches still sorts correctly. A JSON envelope `{consent: {id, purpose{code,text}, fiTypes[], dataRange{from,to}, expiry, accounts[]}, fi: [{fiType: DEPOSIT|CREDIT_CARD|TERM_LOAN|RECURRING_DEPOSIT, account{maskedAccNumber, type, fipName}, summary{currentBalance|creditLimit|principal,rate,tenure...}, transactions[{txnId, type: DEBIT|CREDIT, mode, amount, currentBalance, transactionTimestamp, valueDate, narration, reference}]}]}`. It is loosely modelled on the ReBIT FI schemas (field names borrowed, not validated against XSD). The adapter rejects expired consents, and FI types outside the consent scope. A disclaimer is in code and docs: *we are not an FIU. This mock sits behind the same interface a licensed AA/FIU partner adapter would implement.*
- **statements:** CSV. `config/banks/<bank>.yaml` maps columns (`date`, `narration`, `debit`, `credit` or `amount`+`dr_cr`, `balance`), date format, skip rows and sign conventions. Adding a bank needs no code. PDF: a `PdfStatementAdapter` with the same interface, raising `NotImplementedError("PDF import stubbed")`.
- **sms:** `shared/sms_patterns.yaml` has entries `{id, bank, kind: debit|credit|emi|card_spend, sender_ids[], regex, fields: {1: amount, 2: account_last4, 3: merchant, 4: date}, date_format}`. Fixtures in `shared/sms_fixtures/` pair each sample SMS with its expected output. `app/ingest/sms.py` has two parts: (a) `reference_parse(sms_text)` for tests and demo replay only; (b) the API adapter, which accepts **only** `StructuredSmsTxn {client_ref, pattern_id, sender_id, account_last4, amount_paise, direction, merchant_raw, txn_date, reference?}`. `client_ref` is an opaque on-device id used for idempotency. `reference` is the UPI ref / UTR used for cross-source dedupe. The API schema has no free-text body field, and a test asserts this.
- **manual:** `{account_id?, txn_date, amount_paise, direction, merchant, category?}`. `category_source=user` if given.

## 5. Pipeline (`app/pipeline/`)
1. **Merchant normalisation:** channel-specific regexes turn narrations into `(channel, merchant_raw, counterparty_type, ref)`. Examples: `UPI/DR/412345/SWIGGY/YESB/…`, `NACH-DR-BAJAJFIN-…`, `POS 4321XXXX AMAZON`, `NEFT-HDFC0001234-RAHUL SHARMA-…`. `merchants.yaml` maps aliases to a canonical `merchant_key` (`swiggy`, `netflix`, …). A UPI ID ending in a personal VPA, or NEFT/IMPS to a name that isn't in the merchant map, gives `counterparty_type=person`. The name is replaced with a per-user stable pseudonym (`contact_03`), and the mapping lives only in the DB.
2. **Transfer matching (D3/D4):** pair a debit and a credit across the user's *visible* accounts with the same amount, within 3 days, and a bill-pay or self-transfer signature (or a narration naming the other account's last 4). Both get `category=transfer_self` and a shared `transfer_group_id`. Unmatched legs: a card bill payment to a known-unlinked card → `card_bill_unlinked` (**spend**). A self-transfer out to an unseen account → `transfer_unseen` (**spend**). A self-transfer in from an unseen account → `transfer_in_unseen` (neither income nor spend). Loan EMI debits stay `emi` even when the loan account is linked; liability-account legs (`loan_repayment_in`, `loan_disbursal_out`) are excluded from income and spend. Card purchases, `card_interest` and `fees_charges` (incl. GST on them) on a linked card are always spend. The pipeline **recomputes a user's full history on every ingest** (cheap at this scale), which is what makes the D4 reclassification retroactive. Card bill payments also let the pipeline infer a `known_unlinked` card account (institution + last 4).
3. **De-dupe:** a candidate pair has the same `user_id` and account (matched on masked last-4 + institution), the same `amount_paise` and direction, `|date diff| ≤ 1 day`, and merchant similarity ≥ 0.8 (normalised token-set ratio; equal `merchant_key` passes). Source priority for the canonical record is AA > statement > SMS > manual. Every source is kept in `transaction_sources`. The process is idempotent: re-ingesting the same payload (same `payload_hash`) is a no-op.
4. **Categorise:** (a) `user` overrides; (b) rules: `merchant_key → category` plus channel rules (NACH to a lender → `emi`, SIP ACH to an AMC/BSE → `sip_investment`); (c) the LightGBM fallback. Features: char 2–5-gram TF-IDF on the normalised narration, plus log-amount, direction, channel one-hot, day-of-month. It is trained on seeded synthetic labelled narrations (`hisaab train-categoriser`); the model artefact is written to `backend/artifacts/` along with the train seed and its hash. Evaluation holds out **unseen merchants** (a group split by `merchant_key`) and reports accuracy and macro-F1 in `docs/CATEGORISER.md`, with caveat D15. If ML confidence < 0.5, the category is `other` with `category_source=ml`.

Category set (`categories.yaml`; `essential` flag in brackets; `spend: false` for transfer_self, transfer_in_unseen, loan_repayment_in, loan_disbursal_out, income_*, refund, loan_disbursal, sip_investment): income_salary, income_gig, income_other, refund, loan_disbursal, rent[E], emi[E], sip_investment, groceries[E], utilities[E], telecom[E], education[E], health[E], insurance[E], fuel[E], transport, food_delivery, dining, shopping, ott_subscription, entertainment, travel, cash_withdrawal, fees_charges, card_interest, transfer_self, transfer_person, transfer_unseen, transfer_in_unseen, card_bill_unlinked, loan_repayment_in, loan_disbursal_out, other.

## 6. Intelligence core (`app/engines/`)
All engines are pure: `f(frames, accounts, as_of, params) -> dataclass`. Money outputs are in paise.

### 6.0 The as-of view (D22)
`engines.view.build_view(session, user_id, as_of, accounts=None) -> View` is the only way engines see data:
- **Raw rows:** `txn_date ≤ as_of` and `received_at ≤ as_of 23:59:59 IST`. **Account states** (limit, APR, statement/due days, loan schedules, balances): the latest `account_states` row per account with `as_of_date ≤ as_of` and the same cutoff. **User merchant rules** created by the cutoff.
- The pipeline (§5) runs on that slice, so visibility, transfers and categories are point-in-time.
- An optional `accounts` restriction is used by D24 attribution.
- Per account: `visible_from`, `linked`, `role` (operating / reserve / liability) and `balance_at(as_of)` (last `balance_after` ≤ as_of, else the state balance).
- **Operating account (D6):** the visible deposit account with the most spend debits in the trailing 90 days; ties go to the one that receives salary. Other visible deposit accounts are **reserve**.

### 6.1 Financial engine (FACTS)
Windows: "cycle" = pay cycle between consecutive salary credits when a salary series is detected, otherwise the calendar month. "Trailing" = the last 3 complete cycles.
- `income_monthly` = the **current salary level** (latest amount segment of the salary series, D23) + the trailing-3 median of other income. Without a salary series (irregular), it is the trailing-3-month median of all income, and `income_pattern = irregular`.
- `spend_by_category`: per cycle and for the last 30 days, with share of total.
- `savings_rate` = (income − consumption spend − EMIs − fees/interest) / income. SIPs count as saving.
- `emergency_buffer_months` = liquid balance (all visible savings/current with a known balance, minus recent loan disbursals, D29) / monthly essential spend (trailing-3 median of essential categories, incl. rent, EMIs and **uncategorised**, D21).
- `emi_to_income` = current monthly EMIs (contractual loan schedules ∪ detected EMI series, contract wins) / income. `debt_to_income` = total outstanding (loans + card balance) / annual income.
- `credit_utilisation` = latest statement balance / limit, per card and overall (needs a linked card: limit and statement day). `revolving_balance` = the latest statement **whose due date has passed**, minus payments between its statement date and due date. It is `null` for an unlinked or SMS-only card.
- `recurring` (below). `overlapping_subscriptions`: ≥2 active subscriptions in the same group from `subscriptions.yaml` (e.g. `ott_video`: Netflix, Prime Video, JioHotstar, SonyLIV, Zee5).
- `spending_drift`: per category, last 30 days vs the trailing 3-cycle median. Flagged if > +25% **and** > ₹1,000.
- `data_coverage`: months of history, accounts visible / linked / known, uncategorised share of spend.

### 6.2 Recurring detection
Group non-transfer debits and credits by `(merchant_key, direction)`.
- **Cadence** by gap windows: weekly 5–9 days (≥4 occurrences), monthly 24–38 days with at most one 52–68-day skipped month (≥3 occurrences), quarterly 80–100 days (≥2). Annual: a single charge from a merchant with a configured annual plan (`config/subscriptions.yaml`), amount within 10% of a listed plan price.
- **Amount** (D23): variable bills (utilities, telecom, insurance) qualify within ±40% and are flagged `amount_variable`. Otherwise stable if every amount is within 15% of the median, *or* there is exactly one change-point where both segments are within 5% of their own median and the later segment has ≥2 occurrences. The current amount is the latest segment's median. One trailing odd value is an outlier: the prior level is kept and `pending_change` is flagged.
- **Anchor and next due:** monthly anchor = the most common day-of-month among occurrences (ties go to the earliest). For salary, `next_due` = the anchor in the following month, rolled back over weekends. For debits, the anchor date itself.
- **Active** if the last occurrence is within 1.5 × cadence of `as_of`. `kind` comes from the category (income → `salary`/`income`, `emi`, `sip`, `rent`, `subscription`, `bill`, `fees`, `other`).
- **Contractual schedules** (D5a) from linked loan accounts are merged in, keyed by `lender_merchant_key`, and the contract wins.
- **Irregular income** (Persona B: amount spread fails both tests) is *not* recurring. The forecast treats it through the bootstrap.

### 6.4 Forecast engine (PREDICTIONS)
Modules: `engines/schedule.py` (what is due, when), `engines/forecast.py` (Monte Carlo), `engines/backtest.py` (backtest and confidence).
- **Accounts and horizon:** 45 days. The headline is the operating account (D6). Every other deposit account with scheduled debits is simulated too, for bounce risk. The opening balance is the account's balance at `as_of`, minus earmarked loan cash (D33).
- **Scheduled flows** (`ScheduledFlow`, observed or contractual only):
  - active recurring items on their due dates: salary (weekend roll-back), EMIs (contract wins, D5a), SIPs, rent, bills, subscriptions, and **sweeps** between own accounts. A sweep schedules both legs, so money moving *into* the operating account is modelled (Persona C's household transfer);
  - **card payments** (Phase 3 point 1): for a linked card, the due dates from its statement cycle, and the amount the user **actually pays** = their observed payment ratio (median of the last 3 statements) × the statement balance, on the due date + their observed offset. For an SMS-only card, the observed monthly payment pattern (anchor day; amount = the most recent payment, because a revolving user's payments grow). Card purchases never hit cash directly;
  - a debit that is ≤3 days late is still expected tomorrow.
- **Amount uncertainty:** estimated amounts (variable bills, card payments for statements not yet generated) get a per-path multiplicative factor resampled from that item's own last ≤6 amounts ÷ their median. Fixed amounts (EMIs, rent, subscriptions) have none.
- **Discretionary component:** all *other* flows of the account (not scheduled series, not transfers, not loan legs), as a net amount per day over the last ≤180 days. For each future day we sample one historical day from the same `(day_of_week, dom_bucket)` stratum (1–10 / 11–20 / 21–31). With < 4 days in the stratum we fall back to day-of-week, then to all days. Irregular income (Persona B) lives here. 1,000 paths.
- **Cash-need paths:** every obligation is deducted even when the balance can't cover it, so a negative balance is the shortfall. Order within a day: scheduled credits, then scheduled debits (sweep, EMI, SIP, rent, card, fees, bill, subscription, …), then the discretionary net flow.
- **Seeds and common random numbers:** discretionary draws come from `rng_for(user, "forecast", account)` and are made up front, in a fixed order that doesn't depend on the schedule. Each estimated amount has its own stream keyed by item and date. So a simulation that changes only the schedule sees identical draws. Removing a fixed debit shifts every path by exactly its amount (tested).
- **Outputs:** daily P10/P50/P90 (end of day); `next_income_date` (next primary salary, or +30 days if irregular); `dip_probability` = share of paths whose balance (the opening included) goes below the floor before `next_income_date`; `likely_dip_date` = the mode of the first-dip dates (null if < 5%); `projected_low` = P50 of the per-path minimum over 30 days; P10/P50/P90 on the day before the next income; `bounce_risks` (D18) = per scheduled debit, the share of paths whose balance just before it is below its amount, with name, kind, due date, amount and the `mandate` flag (NACH/ACH EMIs and SIPs); `assumptions` (e.g. earmarked loan cash).
- **Backtest (Phase 3 point 2):** origins every 15 days from day 60 of the operating account's balance history to `as_of − 15`. Each origin cuts the view at that date (no later transactions; account states only if they existed by then), re-detects recurring items and forecasts up to 45 days or until `as_of`. **P10–P90 interval coverage** = the share of actual end-of-day balances inside the band, pooled over origins. This is the number behind confidence and the one we quote to judges. We also report dip calibration (Brier score; mean predicted vs observed dip rate). `docs/FORECAST.md` is generated by `hisaab backtest-report` for every persona and replay step. Personas B and C are untuned.
- **Confidence** (reworked in the Phase 3.5 review; the old ratio read as "right 82% of the time", which was false):
  - The **only percentage** is the measured backtest coverage itself. It is shown in the "why trust this" sheet, never next to the dip probability.
  - **Label** from coverage: **High ≥ 75%, Medium 60–75%, Low < 60%** (Low if there are < 2 backtest origins). History and linkage can only **cap** the label: under 6 months of history or any unlinked known account caps it at Medium; under 3 months or fewer than half the known accounts linked caps it at Low. They never multiply into a new percentage.
  - **Reason line** for the app, next to the label: "band held on 65% of past days, target 80%", plus "; capped at Medium: 1 account not linked" when a cap applied.
  - **One sentence for a judge:** *"Confidence tells you how often our 10-to-90% band actually held on your own past days when we replayed the forecast (target 80%): High from 75%, Medium from 60%, Low below that, and it can't be High with under six months of history or with accounts you haven't linked."*
- **Phase 3.5: balance-aware spending.** Frozen criteria, method and results are in `docs/PHASE_3_5.md`.

### 6.5 Simulation engine (RECOMMENDATIONS)
Modules: `engines/simulate.py` (actions, evaluation, ranking), `engines/projection.py` (12-month roll-forward).

**Two evaluations per action, each against its own baseline:**
1. **Forecast re-run** on a modified schedule with the **same random draws** (common random numbers). This gives bounce-risk and dip changes; removing a fixed debit shifts every path by exactly its amount.
2. **12-month projection**, a deterministic monthly roll-forward of the user's observed behaviour:
   - own liquid money changes by its observed 3-month trend (loan cash excluded);
   - the card follows its own data: statement = revolving × (1 + implied monthly rate) + monthly purchases, and the user pays their observed share;
   - loans amortise on reducing balance.
   The action runs in lockstep with the baseline, so every difference is attributable. Cash the action frees is subject to D26 (`unswept_spend_share` of it is assumed spent unless swept). Money moved out of savings loses savings interest (assumption). Every projected score is labelled by who proposed the action (D10): RECOMMENDATION for Hisaab's actions, a conditional PREDICTION for the user's what-ifs. Pre-salary liquidity and stability pillars are held at their current values.

**Impact** (per action): `annual_impact_paise` (card interest saved + loan interest saved − savings interest foregone + cash freed as is, over 12 months), `monthly_cashflow_paise`, the interest components, `lifetime_cost_paise` (tenure changes), `score_now`, `score_12m_baseline` → `score_12m_with_action` (`score_delta_12m`), revolving and buffer at 12 months, `months_to_clear_card`, `buffer_months_now_after`, `bounce_risk_before/after` (largest NACH/ACH mandate risk in the horizon), `dip_probability_before/after` + `dip_saturated`, a confidence (label + reason; sweeps are capped at Medium), and the `assumptions`.

**Generators** (auto-recommended; D8: what-ifs never are):
| action | Generated when | What it does | Key assumptions |
|---|---|---|---|
| `link_account` | a known card is visible but unlinked (revolving unknown) | D40 data action, ranked first | none |
| `pay_down_card` | revolving > 0 and savings above the cushion (D47: floor + the P10 shortfall on due dates) | D30(c): cash-neutral clearing (or a partial pay-down if savings can't cover it) | card rate from the statement (FACT); savings interest; pay in full once clear |
| `redirect_sweep` | an active savings sweep and revolving > 0 | D30(d): the same monthly amount goes to the card until clear | as above; confidence ≤ Medium |
| `auto_sweep` | X > 0 | D7 + D30(b)/(e): a payday sweep to the **card** while it revolves, else to savings ("increase" if one exists). X = the largest ₹500 step within the observed monthly surplus (D48) raising the largest mandate bounce risk by ≤ 2 pp (and dip ≤ 2 pp unless saturated, D34); irregular income: a share of each payout | `unswept_spend_share`; confidence ≤ Medium |
| `cancel_overlapping_subs` | an overlap group | keep the earliest-seen member (ties: cheapest), cancel the rest | `unswept_spend_share` for the buffer effect; cash freed reported as is |

**What-ifs:** `new_emi {principal, tenure, rate?}`: EMI by reducing balance, rate from the user or `consumer_emi_rate_bps` (stated as an assumption), plus the alternative tenure (×1.5, e.g. 12 → 18 months), and both first-EMI date options (D39). `change_emi_tenure {loan, extra_months}` (D37): offered when bounce risk ≥ 20%, extra interest first. `delay_purchase {amount, from, to}` compares buying later with buying now.

**Ranking:** keep an action if it raises the 12-month score, has a positive rupee impact, or lowers bounce risk. Sort by `score_delta_12m` desc, then `annual_impact_paise` desc, then key. **Combined plan:** the top 3 applied together (one modifier, one schedule), so cash isn't counted twice.

## 7. Events and snapshots (`app/events/`)
- `EventBus` interface: `publish(topic, event)` and `subscribe(topic, group, handler)`. `RedisStreamsBus` is the default (stream `hisaab:events`, consumer group `recompute`, XACK after the snapshot commits). `InProcessBus` is used for tests and is available via `EVENT_BUS=inprocess` (the demo fallback if Redis dies).
- Ingest commits the transactions, then publishes `data.ingested {user_id, source, raw_ids, as_of}`.
- The worker (`events/worker.py`, `hisaab worker`; the demo runs the same code in-process) drains the stream. All pending events for a user **coalesce into one snapshot**, and events are XACKed only after the snapshot commits (unacked events are redelivered). The snapshot holds metrics, score, forecast summary and bands, confidence, backtest, alerts, recommendations and the combined plan. `input_hash` = hash of (user, as_of, ingest watermark, merchant rules, preferences, engine version). If it matches the latest snapshot, nothing new is written (idempotent, tested).
- **Diff** (`diff.py`) of snapshot n−1 → n gives: score/band change, per-pillar deltas, metric deltas, dip-probability change, and recommendations new/removed/rank-changed. Each change gets ≥1 reason code.
- **Attribution (D24):** when the new snapshot has accounts that were not visible in the previous one, every score, pillar and metric delta is split into `behaviour` (previous → new view restricted to previously visible accounts) and `new_data` (restricted → full). The `new_data` part carries `NEW_DATA_REVEALED`, and copy reads "linking your card showed…".
- **Reason codes** (`reasons.py`, deterministic rules):
  `NEW_DATA_REVEALED`, `DEBT_GROWING` (D31), `DEBT_CLEARED`, `ACCOUNT_LINKED`, `HIGH_COST_DEBT_FOUND` (revolving balance or APR ≥ 24% debt newly visible), `INCOME_INCREASED` / `INCOME_DECREASED` (last 2 income credits ≥ 5% above / below the prior median), `NEW_EMI_ADDED` (new EMI recurring item or loan account/disbursal), `EMI_CLOSED`, `SUBSCRIPTION_OVERLAP_FOUND`, `SUBSCRIPTION_CANCELLED`, `SPENDING_DRIFT_UP`, `DIP_RISK_UP` / `DIP_RISK_DOWN` (|Δ| ≥ 10 pp; **withheld while the dip metric is saturated**, D34), `BOUNCE_RISK_UP` / `BOUNCE_RISK_DOWN` (max mandate bounce risk moves ≥ 10 pp), `SCORE_BAND_CHANGED`, `CONFIDENCE_CHANGED` (label change), `REC_ADDED`, `REC_REMOVED`, `REC_RANK_CHANGED`. Each `REC_*` carries `caused_by: [reason codes]`. For an added or re-ranked action, that is its generator's drivers present in this diff. For a removed action, it is its type's removal drivers (e.g. a sweep removed by `NEW_EMI_ADDED` or `BOUNCE_RISK_UP`). Otherwise `OUTRANKED` (it moved down because others arrived) or `DATA_UPDATED`.

### 7.1 Phase 3/4 notes (carried from the Phase 1 review)
- **Debt before savings (D30, D31):** the sweep generator and the new savings-to-card action follow D30. The debt-growth alert (D31) is computed from the per-statement revolving series. Existing recurring sweeps (`kind == "sweep"` in recurring detection, already scheduled by the forecast) are redirected, never re-recommended.
- **Sweep and cash-freeing actions (D7, D25, D26):** `unswept_spend_share = 0.5` is a *stated assumption* returned with every action that frees cash. Reported cash freed is exact. Buffer and score effects use `freed × unswept_spend_share`, except the portion that is swept, which counts in full. The engine never reads generator parameters.
- **Optional estimator (D25):** estimate `unswept_spend_share` per user from how discretionary spend co-moves with balance. Persona A is the known-truth check (~0.6). Until then the parameter stays at the stated 0.5.
- **Backtest honesty (D27):** the rolling-origin backtest reports band hit rate and dip calibration per persona. A's balance-dependent spending is expected to hurt calibration. We report it, and we don't tune it away.

## 9. Synthetic data (`app/demo/`)
`generator.simulate(persona, seed) -> World`: one continuous, seeded, day-by-day simulation of each persona's accounts. `World` renders *raw source payloads* (AA JSON, bank CSVs, raw SMS text) sliced by date and account, so the demo goes through the real pipeline. Cross-source duplicates: ≈15% of Persona A's UPI debits also arrive as SMS, rendered as raw text and parsed by the on-device reference parser into structured SMS transactions. Replay steps are **slices of one simulated world**, so balances stay consistent across steps.

Persona parameters are **generator inputs**, not engine outputs. Engine numbers are whatever the engines compute; they are written to `docs/DEMO_NUMBERS.md` after `make demo`.

- **Behaviour model (all personas).** Discretionary spend is Poisson-count / log-normal-amount streams with weekend and pay-cycle effects. Persona A also has a **spend-down** behaviour: on weekends it spends a share of the operating balance above (obligations due in the next 14 days + a threshold). This is **persona design only**: it is not evidence for `unswept_spend_share` (D25), and engines never read generator parameters. The ledger enforces balances: an overdrawing UPI payment is declined, and an overdrawing mandate bounces with a return charge.
- **A (golden), Pune, salaried.** History 2026-03-21 → 2026-09-20 (inclusive; `as_of` = last day of data). Accounts: salary (operating), savings (reserve), existing loan (AA TERM_LOAN), credit card (**unlinked at T0**, visible only through bill payments from the salary account). The world runs to 2026-11-20. The salary hike (+12%) starts with the Oct 1 credit, and a new personal loan (AA TERM_LOAN with EMI schedule, disbursal credited 2026-11-20, first EMI 2026-12-05) is released only at +3. Net salary ₹92,000 on the 1st (rolled back for weekends). Rent via NEFT to the landlord (a personal name, which exercises PII masking). SIP on the 7th. Three OTT subs (Netflix, Prime Video, JioHotstar). Card: limit ₹1,50,000, pays ~40% of the statement each month, so revolving converges to ~₹40,000 (target band ₹36k–₹44k). Discretionary spend is tuned so balances dip before salary.
- **B, gig worker, Bengaluru.** Weekly irregular platform payouts (CV ~0.35), rent, a phone EMI, no card, 6 months.
- **C, family, Delhi NCR.** Two salaries, home loan + car loan EMIs, quarterly school fees, a card paid in full, 6 months.

**Story checks** (tests on the *generator + engines*, to catch persona drift; they fail loudly instead of us tuning the numbers by hand): A at T0 is in the Fair band; A at +1 has a revolving balance within ₹36k–₹44k; A has 1 overlap group with 3 subs; A at T0 has pre-salary dip probability > 50% (against the D17 floor); A's spend rises and savings rate falls from T0 to +1 (D4).
